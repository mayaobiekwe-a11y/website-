Why We Exist

6.1%

Black women's unemployment rate climbed to 6.1% in April 2026 — nearly double the 3.3% rate for white women, and the steepest monthly jump of any group the Bureau of Labor Statistics tracks.

Source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics

Who We Are

A community where no one rises alone.

Black Women Rising is a community of women who believe no one has to rise alone. We were built on a simple truth: the right person, at the right moment, can change the entire course of a career. So we made a place where that kind of moment happens often, not by chance, but by design.

Founder

Nneka Obiekwe

Nneka spent years as a go-to connector for friends job-hunting, until her network was tapped out and she noticed most of the people asking were Black women who needed community as much as a referral.

In September 2025, she started a WhatsApp chat and posted the link on Threads. Within 24 hours, more than 500 people had joined.

Read Nneka's Story
Why Networking Matters

Most jobs are never posted. They're passed along.

It's often said that the large majority of jobs are filled through referrals and personal networks rather than cold applications — and with AI resume screeners now filtering out qualified candidates before a human ever sees them, who you know matters more than ever. That's why community isn't a nice-to-have in this job market. It's the strategy.

It's also why we partnered with Mobi, an AI-assisted networking app that helps you build the real relationships a cold application never will.
